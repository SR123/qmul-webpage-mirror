Logic & proof complexity

When does one counting principle imply another?

My work on modular counting characterises implications between counting principles through the prime factors of their moduli.

Søren Riis · Annals of Pure and Applied Logic, 1997

Counting and the complexity of proofs →

Information flow

What can a network infer from local information?

Graph guessing games connect local constraints with network coding. With Maximilien Gadouleau, I developed constructions linking guessing graphs, graph entropy, and communication.

Gadouleau & Riis · IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 2011

Guessing numbers and network coding →

Collective choice

How large can a Condorcet domain be?

For eight alternatives, the maximum is 224 orders, with a unique example up to isomorphism: a result with Charles Leedham-Green and Klas Markström.

Leedham-Green, Markström & Riis · Social Choice and Welfare, 2024

The structure of collective preferences →

Games & learning

Does winning reveal understanding?

With Bei Zhou, I use impartial games to examine the gap between competitive performance and broader mastery in reinforcement learning.

Zhou & Riis · Machine Learning, 2026
